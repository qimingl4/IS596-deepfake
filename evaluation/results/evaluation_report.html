Executive Summary

This report presents the results of a user evaluation study of Deep-Guard Agent v2.0, an automated deepfake detection system based on audio-visual articulatory analysis. Ten university students (5 technical, 5 non-technical backgrounds) participated in a 60-minute within-subjects study comprising three video analysis tasks, a System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire, custom Likert scales, and a semi-structured interview.

Overall: Deep-Guard Agent achieved a mean SUS score of 70.0/100 ("Above Average"), with system-assisted verdict agreement of 85% — a +30 percentage-point improvement over the unaided baseline (55%). The Legal Report was rated most useful by law and journalism students (M = 5.8/7). The primary usability concern was unfamiliar technical vocabulary (4/10 participants).

Key Findings
Finding 01
Above-Average Usability, Uneven by Background
Mean SUS = 70.0 ("Above Average"). CS/IS users rated it 84/100 vs. non-technical 61/100 — a 23-point gap indicating onboarding friction for non-experts.
Finding 02
AI Assistance Significantly Improves Accuracy
System-assisted agreement rate of 85% vs. unaided baseline 55% — a +30 percentage-point improvement, demonstrating clear value of the AI-generated report.
Finding 03
Subtle Deepfakes Remain Challenging
Task 3 (Suspicious video) had the lowest agreement rate (60%) and highest task time (339s). Borderline cases expose the current limits of AI-assisted detection for lay users.
Finding 04
Legal Report Most Valued by Target Audience
Law & Journalism students rated Legal Report usefulness at 5.8/7, vs. CS/IS at 4.6/7 — confirming alignment with non-technical users who are the primary beneficiaries.
Finding 05
Technical Vocabulary is Primary Barrier
4/10 participants reported difficulty with terms like "cosine similarity" and "phoneme mismatch". This is the single most actionable usability issue.
Finding 06
Strong Familiarity–Usability Correlation
Spearman ρ = 0.97 between AI familiarity and SUS score. Users with higher AI experience consistently rate the system as more usable, confirming that domain knowledge bridges the gap.

Recommendations
  • 1
    Add inline glossary / tooltips for technical terms (cosine similarity, phoneme mismatch, discrepancy threshold). Directly addresses the top-ranked concern (4/10 participants). Estimated impact: +5–10 SUS points for non-technical users.
  • 2
    Add a plain-English summary card at the top of the Report tab — one paragraph before the technical evidence — so non-technical users can understand the conclusion without reading detailed metrics.
  • 3
    Annotate the Discrepancy Timeline chart with axis labels, a legend, and a sentence explaining what high vs. low scores mean. 4/10 participants found the current chart unclear.
  • 4
    Add a first-use onboarding walkthrough (3-step tooltip tour covering: upload, analyze, read report) to reduce time-on-task for new users and close the technical/non-technical gap.
  • 5
    Show a progress indicator during analysis. 4/10 participants mentioned speed as a concern. A progress bar with estimated remaining time would reduce perceived wait time without changing actual processing speed.
